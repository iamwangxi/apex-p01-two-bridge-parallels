# Scope, attribution and limits of the evidence

[Contents](../README.md) · [Bibliography and checked editions](../sources/bibliography.md)

The [main theorem](main-theorem.md) proves $`|Y(L)|\searrow|X(L)|\searrow|MS(L)|`$ for **all integer framings** of the finite connected-sum/cable class generated by nontrivial two-bridge and torus knots; all integer-framed parallels of arbitrary nontrivial fibred knots with nonzero orientation sum; and all integer-framed balanced doubles of arbitrary nontrivial fibred knots. All genera and disconnected spanning systems remain in the vertex set.

The complete proof pages are [zero-framed clusters](zero-framed-clusters.md), [integer-framed two-bridge clusters](framed-clusters.md), [zero-framed recursive/nonbalanced fibred companions](recursive-and-fibred.md), and [framed recursive parallels and fibred doubles](framed-recursive-and-fibred.md). The comparison input is the fixed public [CW, Part 7, Theorem D]. Tautness is established before applying [PS, Theorem 1.1]. For recursive companions, or arbitrary fibred companions, $`fd\ne0`$ gives $`X=Y=MS(L)`$ and the stated Euler formula, without forcing a point.

For arbitrary balanced zero-framed fibred clusters, [Theorem C](framed-recursive-and-fibred.md) retains the entire separating-companion subcomplex and strongly retracts the regular branch onto its annular intersection. This reduces the full problem exactly to contractibility of the separating subcomplex; it does not prove that subcomplex contractible for every fibred knot with four or more actual components. The two-boundary compression barrier cannot be assumed there: §10 gives an actual fibred example with disconnected separating vertices, shared whole components and a connected vertex adjacent to a disconnected one. The example lies in the recursive class, so is not a noncontractibility example. Section 7 states sufficient hereditary path/root conditions; fibredness alone is not asserted to supply them.

The detailed cone, Farey, disk, odd-signature and integer edge classifications concern **two oppositely oriented zero-framed parallels**. The two-terminal obstruction is confined to that specialization. Larger clusters retain the nonparallel multi-boundary pieces it excluded. The explicit two-parallel simplex description is not a classification of all geometric simplices.

**Uncovered cases.** General Montesinos/arborescent companions outside the proved recursive or fibred ranges are not covered. Balanced clusters of four or more parallels of arbitrary fibred knots require the additional stated inputs for contractibility. For arbitrary companions, a balanced framing excluded from all essential boundary slopes forces empty companion cut; nonzero-framed balanced doubles also follow from the official linking-indecomposable theorem. No arbitrary-link theorem, exhaustive JSJ criterion or complete simplicial classification for the wider ranges is asserted.

## Established inputs and comparison with the literature

Hatcher–Thurston [HT] already classify incompressible surfaces in a two-bridge **companion knot exterior**. That classification, its all-even Seifert-surface consequences and its pillowcase coordinates are inputs, not new companion-knot theorems here. The cluster theorem concerns the parallel-link exterior; its path closure retains nonparallel whole-system data that a list of boundary slopes alone would not provide.

Sakuma [S] proves the minimal-genus geometric $`MS`$ complex for special arborescent links is a finite-dimensional ball [Theorem 3.3; Propositions 3.9 and 3.11, pp.873, 876–877]. The full incompressible $`IS`$ contractibility appears in Conjecture 0.2, p.862, and is not that proved theorem. For the two-opposite-parallel specialization, his semi-oriented link class does not contain those oriented links. Indeed his defining tree plumbs unknotted-core annuli with nonzero integer weights [pp.866–867]. The target link has a spanning annulus with $`\chi=0`$, which is minimal because its components are nontrivial knots and no spanning disk component exists. If it were one of his links, Theorem 2.3 [pp.869–870] would make that annulus isotopic to a canonical tree surface. For $`v`$ annuli and $`v-1`$ plumbings its Euler characteristic is $`1-v`$, forcing $`v=1`$. Its nonzero-twist annulus then has nonzero linking number, contrary to zero framing. Thus neither the link class nor the full vertex and edge range matches. Contractibility of the companion's geometric $`MS(C)`$ is already known and is not counted as a contribution. Independently of his special-arborescent contractibility results, Sakuma's Proposition 4.9(1)–(2), p.883, explicitly applies to the geometric $`IS(L)`$ of every non-split link. Its flagness and unlabelled-union uniqueness are inputs of [CW], as detailed in §19. The exclusion from his special link class does not exclude the present link from that proposition.

Roberts [R, Theorem 1, p.1; Theorem 17 and Corollary 18, p.13] counts many inequivalent minimal-genus spanning surfaces for certain arborescent **knots**. His equivalence permits the boundary knot to move in the three-sphere [Definition 3, p.2]. It supplies neither the target two-component parallel-link classification nor its whole cyclic complex. Surface counts alone do not determine its edges or contractibility.

Kakimizu [K05] allows disconnected componentwise incompressible spanning surfaces, so it would be inaccurate to describe that paper as minimal-genus-only. Its main Theorems A and C [pp.48–49] classify prime **knots** of ten or fewer crossings and their geometric $`IS`$ complexes. The general plumbing tools in §§3 and 5 have additional hypotheses, including specified minimal-genus initial plumbings; they do not give the present zero-framed parallel-link theorem. Its two-component annulus example [Lemma 6.4, p.84] has unknotted core and nonzero twist, hence nonzero linking number, and is a different link. The connectivity theorem recalled on p.48 is not a contractibility theorem for arbitrary links. The three read comparison papers therefore do not contain or directly imply the stated result.

This comparison is limited to the specified read works. Kakimizu's 1991 BLMS paper, *Doubled knots with infinitely many incompressible spanning surfaces*, has not been read and is not ruled out by its title. No exhaustive priority certification is claimed.

## Dependence on the target and other theorems

The target is the official 11 September 2026 competition paper [C], not a later author revision. Its Introduction, printed p.2, supplies the general-link question and identifies the failed hypothesis of Lemma 4.9. The passage from the full, possibly disconnected cyclic object to its original geometric counterpart uses [CW]. The balanced-double proof additionally applies the target's exchange theorem only inside the connected separating sector, after proving a compression barrier and retaining all other vertices through the regular envelope. The citation is fixed at commit `f6f44253f2ed539a74bf4cfdc99fed299e386546`, with the statement in `proof/part7.md` and the proof in `proof/part7-2.md`, §9. This is a mathematical dependency, not a new proof of the general comparison in this repository.

The proof uses [B] for marked product regions, minimal representative width and the explicit all-incompressible extension in §10. The cited comparison [CW] additionally uses [S, Proposition 4.9(1)–(2)] in its stated all-incompressible range; it is not inferred from taut-only statements of [PS]. The detailed two-parallel cyclic proof uses [PS, §§2–3] for complement blocks and simplified intersection. The cluster proofs additionally invoke [PS, Theorem 1.1, p.1490] for the **identified taut cyclic endpoint**, including norm-zero cases. They do not extend its taut projection to arbitrary incompressible vertices. The essential-cut and verticalisation inputs are [H3, Lemma 1.11 and Proposition 1.12], together with the elementary arguments supplied in §3.

The standard-library scripts check exact-rational expansion lists, bounded move orbits, raw rectangle restrictions, coupled rooted heights, folding/sign obstructions, full finite flag complexes and explicit domination deletions. Independent implementations and saved deletion certificates are included. All three infinite families have separate symbolic exhaustion proofs in [§10](infinite-family.md). The script does not certify marked three-dimensional product recognition, ambient isotopies or the contraction.
