## HT — Hatcher–Thurston

**A. Hatcher and W. Thurston.** *Incompressible surfaces in 2-bridge knot complements*. *Inventiones Mathematicae* **79** (1985), 225–246. Public acquisition route: [Hatcher's author page](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/), papers collection.

Acquisition: supplied public author-copy PDF and extracted text. The mathematical source checks and adversarial review used Theorem 1(b)–(e), p.228; Propositions 1–2 and the Seifert-surface corollary including the fibre clause, p.230; the pillowcase arc classification, p.232; the whole-system proof, pp.233–239; the horizontal regular-neighbourhood frontier and doubled weights, p.240; boundary/Euler counts, pp.243–244; the abstract covering statement, p.244; and component foldings and the exact all-even count, p.245. The branched double-cover lens-space observation is on p.225.

Original page checks in those records included pp.227–228, 230, 232, 240, 243–245. In particular the admissibility condition is absolute coefficient **at least** two, including the endpoint move clause; some extracted inequalities lose the equality sign. The proof here uses the same-sign double moves only at coefficients equal to plus or minus two. This edition is the classification input, not a new classification of the companion's Seifert surfaces. The cluster proofs additionally use the full bounded-system scope, pp.227–230, the complete $`\pm2`$ moves and their supports in Figs.24–25, p.241, and the transported squares and band reversals in Figs.26–28 and 30–31, pp.242–245. Proposition 2, p.230, supplies arbitrary finite integral boundary slopes; its use is not restricted to longitudinal one-boundary surfaces.

**Remark (the p.230 aggregate count).** The parenthetical aggregate orbit count on printed p.230 conflicts with the complete moves in Theorem 1(e), p.228. On $`[3,2,4,-2]`$ those moves preserve $`x_1-x_2`$ and give $`2N+1`$ bounded orbits, rather than $`N+1`$. More generally an intervening free block of length $`L`$ gives $`NL+1`$ orbits. [Lemma 14.1](../proof/bounded-parameters.md) proves this directly inside the bounded parameter box. The present proof uses Theorem 1(e) and that bounded argument, and does not use the aggregate aside. Original-page checks for the extended argument also covered printed pp.227, 228, 230, 231, 241, 242, 244 and 245, including Figs.3, 24–28 and 30–31. The independently reproduced p.231 counts for the eight carriers of $`K_{5/13}`$ are $`7,8,4,4,8,8,12,8`$.

## PS — Przytycki–Schultens, journal edition

**Piotr Przytycki and Jennifer Schultens.** *Contractibility of the Kakimizu complex and symmetric Seifert surfaces*. *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* **364** (2012), no.3, 1489–1508. [DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-2011-05465-6](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-2011-05465-6).

Acquisition: supplied journal PDF and extracted text. PDF SHA-256: `f00eba68abe0dfcab5cd4297e80c0cd454c7433112f7a373a72a609cce25157e`.

**Theorem 1.1, p.1490**, is used in the cluster proofs only after the endpoint is proved to be the taut cyclic complex $`MS(E,\gamma,\alpha)`$. Its hypotheses are checked: non-split link exterior, irreducibility, boundary irreducibility, primitive nonseparating actual boundary curves and the link-exterior relative homology condition. This includes balanced norm-zero endpoints and disconnected spanning systems. It is not a theorem about every incompressible vertex.

The detailed two-parallel proof also uses §§2–3, pp.1492–1495: whole open complement blocks, representative and class widths, the disconnected-surface warning, almost-transverse position, product simplification and coincident components. The source records visually checked these pages. No taut projection from §§4–7 is applied to non-taut vertices. The stated contractibility conclusion is used solely through Theorem 1.1 on the identified taut endpoint. The all-incompressible minimum-width application is made via the explicitly stated Banks extension above.

Acquisition: supplied version-of-record PDF from the Osaka institutional archive and extracted text. The special-arborescent results are used for literature comparison. Separately, Proposition 4.9(1)–(2), p.883, is an explicit input to the component-wall comparison [CW] used for the geometric conclusion here. Checked in the novelty review: Conjecture 0.2 and the announced minimal-genus scope, p.862; surface conventions, p.863; nonzero-weight unknotted annulus plumbing and semi-orientation, pp.866–867; Theorem 2.3, pp.869–870; geometric simplex definitions, pp.871–872; Theorem 3.3, p.873; Propositions 3.9 and 3.11, pp.876–877; geometric flagness and uniqueness of the isotopy type of the unlabelled simultaneous union, Proposition 4.9(1)–(2), p.883. Both clauses explicitly include $`IS(L)`$ for every non-split link; they impose no special-arborescent or minimal-genus restriction. The definitions on p.863 allow disconnected surfaces with no closed components and ordinary isotopy in the exterior; pp.871–872 define simultaneous whole disjoint representatives. These source-text ranges were rechecked offline for the geometric extension. The review visually checked pp.866, 869–871, 873 and 877. The proved complex is geometric minimal-genus MS; IS contractibility remains in the stated conjecture there. See the [scope note](../proof/scope.md) for the oriented-link-class exclusion.

## K05 — Kakimizu

**Osamu Kakimizu.** *Classification of the incompressible spanning surfaces for prime knots of 10 or less crossings*. *Hiroshima Mathematical Journal* **35** (2005), 47–92.

Acquisition: supplied public journal PDF and extracted text; public bibliographic route is the journal's 2005 volume 35 archive. Literature comparison: the possibly disconnected, componentwise incompressible convention, p.47; Theorems A and C and geometric IS, pp.48–49; Proposition 2.1, p.56, and Proposition 2.3, p.57; the hypotheses of Theorems 3.12 and 3.15, pp.59, 68, 70–71, and Theorems 5.4 and 5.10, pp.74–75, 78–79; Lemma 6.4 and Figure 6.3, p.84, the nonzero-twist unknotted-core annulus example. The general plumbing tools are not automatically applicable; their minimal-genus starting surfaces have negative Euler characteristic, whereas the target oriented link has a spanning annulus of characteristic zero.

## CW — Component-wall comparison

*Incompressible spanning surface complexes of links: a general comparison theorem and contractibility for large classes of graph links*. Public repository `iamwangxi/apex-p01-seifert-fibred-links`, fixed commit `f6f44253f2ed539a74bf4cfdc99fed299e386546`.

Used: Part 7, Theorem D ([statement, page 1, §1](https://github.com/iamwangxi/apex-p01-seifert-fibred-links/blob/f6f44253f2ed539a74bf4cfdc99fed299e386546/proof/part7.md#1-objects-and-statements)); [proof, page 2, §9](https://github.com/iamwangxi/apex-p01-seifert-fibred-links/blob/f6f44253f2ed539a74bf4cfdc99fed299e386546/proof/part7-2.md#9-a-component-wall-retraction-independent-of-jsj-slices). For every nonempty oriented non-split link in the three-sphere, on the full reduced all-incompressible vertex set, the identity gives $`X\subseteq Y`$ and $`|X|`$ is a strong deformation retract of $`|Y|`$. No graph-manifold or tautness hypothesis is required. It does not assert contractibility by itself.

The proof uses the printed all-incompressible inputs [S, Proposition 4.9(1)–(2)] and [B, Proposition 4.10 and its explicit §10 extension], with [B, Proposition 4.2(1), Remark 4.4] for component packets. The two-parallel hypothesis checks are in [§19](../proof/geometric-comparison.md); the wider cluster checks are in [the main theorem](../proof/main-theorem.md) and its three proof pages. The fixed public theorem is cited as an external mathematical result; its proof is not reproduced as a self-contained appendix here. The supplied local pages were read offline; this revision does not claim a new online verification of the remote commit.
